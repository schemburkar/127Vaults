SECURITY APPROACH

Encrypted on your device. Synchronized on your terms.

Your clients encrypt vault contents before synchronization. Your self-hosted server stores encrypted records so your devices can catch up whenever they reconnect.

THE TRUST BOUNDARY

Encrypt before sync.

Vault encryption and decryption happen on authorized clients. The server coordinates encrypted changes and device registration; it does not need readable vault contents to synchronize your devices.

Sync storage is persistent, not a temporary relay. Encrypted records remain on your server so a device can synchronize while your other devices are offline. With Self-Hosted, you operate that server and its backups. 127Vaults does not host your vault data under a self-hosted license.

LAYERS OF PROTECTION

More than a master password.

01

Password + Secret Key

Your master password and a randomly generated Secret Key contribute to protecting your vault keys. Keep both private, along with your recovery material.

02

Authenticated encryption

XChaCha20-Poly1305 encrypts vault records and authenticates their contents. Argon2id makes password-based key derivation use both memory and computation.

03

Encrypted local copies

Desktop and CLI clients retain encrypted copies for offline access to synchronized data. Device registration controls which clients can participate.

CLEAR BOUNDARIES

Know what you trust.

The server retains operational metadata such as accounts, registered devices and synchronization state. Encryption does not hide all metadata. An unlocked device handles readable secrets, so its operating system and the applications you copy secrets into remain part of your security.

The web vault requires trusting the server that delivers its code. A compromised server could alter that code before unlock. Revoking a device cannot erase secrets it has already copied.
