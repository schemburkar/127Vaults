## 6. Security, data and expiry

Customer owns its data and manages credentials, backups, recovery material,
deployment and lawful use. Licensor receives no ownership of Customer vault
contents. Customer must keep recovery material separately from encrypted backups.
For self-hosted licenses, expiry ends ordinary operational rights and starts a
30-calendar-day read-only grace period. During that period, Customer may use
supported read and export functions, including on previously synchronized
offline clients, solely to retrieve its data or arrange renewal. This is not an
additional write or synchronization entitlement. Going offline, reinstalling,
changing a clock, or downloading again does not restart or extend the period.
After that period, continued Software operation requires a renewed license or
separate written authorization. Customer retains ownership of its data and may
retain encrypted files and backups; independently available rights are unaffected.

Expiry itself does not delete or alter self-hosted encrypted data, key envelopes
or backups and does not revoke encryption keys. Installing a valid replacement
license removes the licensing restriction for its covered scope; it does not
guarantee decryption, data restoration, compatibility or successful synchronization.
Customer still needs intact data, required credentials, recovery material and a
compatible installation. Licensor does not promise indefinite recovery, retention
of old recovery services, or support after the grace period. Any later recovery
assistance requires a separate agreement and remains subject to feasibility.

These are contractual use limits, not a representation that every offline client
technically stops at expiry. Existing clients may retain local access or queue
edits that the server refuses. Before adoption, document and verify the actual
read/export and offline behavior for each covered client; provide a usable export
path during the stated window. Do not describe a server write guard as a complete
client lock. Cloud retention and export are governed by section 6A.

## 6A. Cloud service and privacy

For an accepted Cloud order, Licensor operates the server infrastructure and
manages server updates. Customer manages its users, authorized devices, credentials
and recovery material. Customer retains ownership of vault contents. Vault contents
are encrypted on clients; hosting does not give Licensor the customer's decryption
credentials or a promise that Licensor can recover lost secrets. Client-side
encryption does not mean that contact, billing, device or service metadata is absent.

Licensor may keep new Cloud registrations closed, mark the offer Coming Soon,
or stop accepting new orders. A listing or access request alone creates no Cloud
service entitlement. Closing new registrations does not cancel or reduce an
existing accepted order or excuse its service obligations.

The Cloud order must include a service/privacy schedule identifying the contracting
operator and privacy contact, hosting region, data categories and purposes,
processors, security responsibilities, incident-notification arrangements, and
applicable data-processing terms. It must disclose contact/account-data retention,
rights-request procedures, international transfers where relevant, and deletion
and backup-retention periods. Publish the applicable privacy notice at collection;
this agreement does not replace that notice or make blanket consent a condition
for unrelated processing.

Before accepting a Cloud order, specify a finite post-expiry read/export window,
its start and end, advance notices, suspension behavior, hosted-data deletion date
and backup purge schedule. State any legally required retention exceptions.
There is no indefinite hosted retention or recovery entitlement; do not invent a
deletion deadline from silence. Customer must receive the agreed opportunity to
export before scheduled deletion. Cloud signups remain gated until this schedule
and the supported export mechanism are complete.

agreement and removes operational copies, except legally required archival
copies and rights independently granted elsewhere. Customer retains its data.
Confidentiality, accrued payment, ownership, applicable liability and dispute
provisions survive as their nature requires. The order must define any
